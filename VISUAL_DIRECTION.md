# Belt Theory — Authentic Archival Visual Direction

## Core principle

Belt Theory is a documentary-style wrestling research publication. Its visual authority must come from **real wrestling history**, not synthetic wrestlers, fabricated faces, fake arenas or generic sports imagery.

The site should feel like a premium long-form sports magazine, historical documentary and data-journalism project built from authentic source material.

## Non-negotiable rule

**Do not use AI-generated human faces, fake wrestlers, fabricated crowds, invented championship belts or synthetic historical scenes.**

AI-generated imagery may be used only for clearly abstract, nonrepresentational texture or utility graphics when no real person, event, logo, belt, venue or historical moment is implied. The default is still to avoid it.

## Primary image categories

Use real, rights-cleared imagery from the following categories:

- Wrestlers in matches, entrances, celebrations, interviews and title presentations
- Champions holding the actual championship being discussed
- Managers, promoters, bookers, owners and authority figures
- Fans, crowd signs, queues, concourses and arena atmosphere
- Rings, stages, entrance sets, production trucks and venue exteriors
- Stadium and arena establishing shots
- Real championship belts and title plates
- Press conferences, media scrums and backstage environments
- Historical event photography
- Promotional posters, programs, tickets, newspaper pages and magazine spreads when reuse rights permit
- Archival television stills only when legally licensed or defensible for limited editorial commentary
- Promotion logos and event marks as contextual editorial identifiers, not decorative wallpaper
- Venue maps, seating diagrams, timelines and real historical documents
- Photographer contact sheets and sequential photo essays when available

## Preferred source hierarchy

1. Original photography commissioned for Belt Theory
2. Public-domain archives
3. Creative Commons photography with compatible commercial reuse terms
4. Promotion-released Creative Commons media collections
5. Properly licensed editorial-photo services
6. Direct permission from photographers, wrestlers, promotions or rights holders
7. Limited fair-use excerpts only after an editorial and legal review

Do not scrape social media, hotlink unlicensed images, remove watermarks or treat search-engine availability as permission.

## Editorial presentation

### Photography leads the page

The layout should be built around the image rather than treating imagery as a background texture.

Use:

- Full-bleed opening photographs
- Split-screen era comparisons
- Photo essays and chronological image sequences
- Documentary captions with person, event, place and date
- Photographer and license credits visible on the page
- Narrow data rails beside photographs
- Pull quotes placed in negative space, never across faces
- Charts that visually inherit the photograph’s crop, era and tonal palette
- Large archival details: belts, hands, signs, turnbuckles, tickets, programs and crowd reactions
- Before/after and then/now pairings
- Contact-sheet grids for reigns, eras and title pathways

Avoid generic dashboard walls, repeated card grids and decorative images that do not add evidence or historical context.

### Era treatment

Images should retain their historical character.

- Territory and early national eras: restrained monochrome, warm paper tones, visible print grain
- 1980s–1990s television era: saturated broadcast color, halftone and CRT-inspired details used sparingly
- 2000s arena era: flash photography, hard contrast, tabloid and magazine energy
- Modern era: cleaner cinematic crops, deep blacks and accurate promotion color accents

Do not recolor every image into the same preset. Preserve recognizable ring, belt, wardrobe and event colors.

## Data integration

Data should explain the photograph and the photograph should humanize the data.

Examples:

- A champion portrait paired with reign length, prior-title path and durability
- A title presentation photo paired with lineage status and governance notes
- A stadium photograph paired with the promotion’s system score and era dates
- A tag-team action photo paired with team concentration and world-title conversion
- A women’s title celebration paired with first-time champion durability
- An owner or booker portrait paired with an era-specific architecture chart

Charts should not cover eyes, faces, belts or critical action. Use adjacent columns, caption bands, translucent edge panels or separate evidence frames.

## Promotion identity

Each promotion should be recognizable through actual historical material rather than a fictional color treatment alone.

- WWE: arena scale, title presentations, generational ownership, broadcast spectacle
- AEW: modern stadium scale, crowd identity, faction and team photography
- TNA: six-sided-ring history, X-Division action, Knockouts and changing visual identities
- WCW: Nitro-era photography, Turner-era production, nWo-era crowd and roster images
- ECW: intimate venues, audience proximity, Paul Heyman, core champions and physical belt photography

## Logos and trademarks

Logos may appear for editorial identification, chronology and comparison. They must not imply endorsement, partnership or official status.

- Keep logos secondary to the editorial photography
- Preserve historical logo versions when discussing specific eras
- Do not redraw logos with AI
- Do not place promotion logos on merchandise or sell them as Belt Theory branding
- Include a clear independent-publication disclaimer

## Captions and credits

Every published image requires:

- Subject or scene
- Event or context
- Date or approximate era
- Photographer or creator
- Source collection
- License or permission basis
- Modification disclosure when cropped, toned or composited
- Permanent source URL in the image-rights ledger

The visible page may use a concise credit; the full record belongs in `IMAGE_RIGHTS_LEDGER.csv`.

## People and historical accuracy

- Never fabricate a person’s presence at an event
- Never use a later photograph as though it depicts an earlier era without labeling it clearly
- Do not visually imply a title reign, match or affiliation that did not occur
- Do not combine unrelated people into fake historical scenes
- Distinguish recreation, commemorative appearance and actual period photography
- Verify names, dates, venues, titles and event identities before publishing

## Visual details to add

- Photo captions that function like mini source notes
- Archival date stamps and event-location labels
- Photographer credit drawer or credits footer
- Image-source badges: Public domain, CC BY, CC BY-SA, Licensed, Courtesy
- A visible corrections link on every long-form report
- Photo-led section dividers instead of synthetic belt artwork
- Real crowd and venue images to establish scale between promotions
- Historical documents and belt close-ups for quieter analytical sections
- Accessible alt text describing the editorial meaning, not only the objects shown
- Responsive crops manually reviewed for desktop and mobile
- Focal-point metadata so faces and belts are not cut off on small screens
- Loading, dimensions and modern formats optimized without erasing archival texture

## What the site should feel like

The visual reference is not a fantasy wrestling poster. It is a cross between:

- a prestige sports documentary
- an archival magazine feature
- a museum-quality historical exhibition
- modern data journalism
- an independent wrestling photo essay

The data remains rigorous, but the human history becomes visible.
